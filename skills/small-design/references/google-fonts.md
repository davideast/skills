# Google Fonts by classification

A working set of Google Fonts, grouped by classification. Use it in passes 2 and 3 of `type-decision.md`.

Each classification says **what it signals**, because that is how it is chosen. Each font lists its available weights, italics and variable axes (taken from Google Fonts metadata), followed by its character and where it fits. Weights and axes change over time; confirm on Google Fonts before relying on an unusual weight.

## Humanist Sans

**Structure:** sans serifs built on the proportions of handwriting with a broad pen. Strokes vary a little in thickness, the openings of letters like `c`, `e` and `s` are wide, and `a` and `g` usually keep their two-storey forms.

**Signals:** a person speaking plainly. Warmth, openness and trust without formality.

**Reach for it when** the interface should feel hosted or conversational. The open shapes stay distinct at small sizes, so these are safe for dense interface text.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Alegreya Sans | 7 weights, 100–900, italics | Pen-made feel with slightly flared stroke endings and an uneven, lively rhythm. Signals storytelling and craft. Suits cultural and editorial subjects; too much flavour for a neutral product. |
| Fira Sans | 9 weights, 100–900, italics | Wide openings, sturdy strokes and faintly squared curves, first drawn for a phone operating system. Signals capable and straightforward. The large family supports a full hierarchy from one face. |
| Source Sans 3 | Variable 200–900, italics | Slender and upright with restrained details and a light texture. Signals calm, clear and courteous. Good for long interface text and documentation; quiet at display sizes. |
| Inria Sans | 300, 400, 700, italics | Humanist proportions with rational, slightly squared curves, made for a research institute. Signals measured and academic but personable. Three weights limit the hierarchy. |
| Lato | 5 weights, 100–900, italics | Classical proportions with softened details. It is so widely deployed that it reads as a familiar default. Use when familiarity is the goal; it will not differentiate. |
| Open Sans | Variable 300–800, width 75–100, italics | Upright, wide and generously spaced. Signals plain and accessible. Reliable legibility with no distinct voice, for the same reason as Lato. |

## Neo/Grotesque and Gothic Sans

**Structure:** descended from nineteenth-century commercial sans serifs and their rationalised mid-twentieth-century successors. Stroke thickness is nearly uniform, letter openings are tighter, terminals tend to end horizontally, and the rhythm is compact and even.

**Signals:** neutrality and institution. The content matters; the type claims nothing.

### Clean and Simple

**Signals:** impartial, systematic, self-effacing. The voice of a public service or a default product. The risk is anonymity.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Archivo | Variable 100–900, width 62–125, italics | Grotesque with slightly squared curves and a width axis running from condensed to extended. Signals assertive and editorial, sporty when wide and bold. One family can supply narrow labels and wide display. |
| Inter | Variable 100–900, optical size 14–32, italics | Drawn for screens: tall lowercase, careful small-size spacing, many numeral and alternate features. Signals contemporary software. So common in product interfaces that it reads as "default app"; choose it for invisibility, avoid it for identity. |
| Public Sans | Variable 100–900, italics | Plain, sturdy and slightly wide, developed for a government design system. Signals civic, impartial and trustworthy. Suits forms, labels and anywhere the type should stay out of the way. |

### Quirky/Friendly

**Signals:** approachable and independent: a small studio, not an institution. The skeleton is still plain, but a few deliberate eccentricities give it a personality that shows in headlines and recedes in text.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Karla | Variable 200–800, italics | Compact, with idiosyncratic letter shapes and a slightly irregular rhythm. Signals indie, casual and a little offbeat. Good for small brands and short text; the oddities are noticeable in headlines. |
| Work Sans | Variable 100–900, italics | Soft round forms with relaxed spacing and small flicks on letters like `a`, `g` and `t`. Middle weights are tuned for screen text, the extremes for display. Signals approachable and unpretentious. Works as a single-family system from title to caption. |

### Condensed/Expanded

**Signals:** width used as voice. Narrow letters signal urgency, headlines and density. Wide letters signal confidence and space. Mostly for display and labels.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Archivo | Variable 100–900, width 62–125, italics | See above. The width axis covers both ends of this group. |
| Bebas Neue | 400 only, capitals only, no italics | Tall, narrow, uniform capitals. Signals posters, sport and film titles. Display only; there is no lowercase. |
| Oswald | Variable 200–700, no italics | Condensed gothic in the manner of newspaper headline type, redrawn for screens, with a lowercase. Signals news and impact. For headlines, labels and large numbers. |

## Geometric Sans

**Structure:** built from the circle, the straight line and the square, with little variation in stroke thickness.

### Clean/Simple

**Signals:** modern, tidy and optimistic. Less personal than a humanist sans, friendlier than a grotesque. Where `a` and `g` are single-storey, small text gets harder to read.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| DM Sans | Variable 100–1000, optical size 9–40, italics | Low-contrast and compact with an optical size axis. Signals tidy, modern and quietly confident. Suits both interface and marketing text. |
| Figtree | Variable 300–900, italics | Simple shapes with gently softened curves; stays readable small. Signals light and uncomplicated, cheerful in bold weights. Suits product interfaces and onboarding. |
| Plus Jakarta Sans | Variable 200–800, italics | Tall lowercase, wide round counters and slightly sharp, distinctive terminals. Signals fresh and polished. Works for headlines and interface text. |
| Jost | Variable 100–900, italics | In the tradition of 1920s German geometric type: pointed apexes, a circular `o`, long ascenders, single-storey `a`. Signals modernist and architectural. Best for display and short capital labels; the low lowercase hurts small text. |
| Rubik | Variable 300–900, italics | Sturdy, squarish forms with slightly rounded corners and a wide stance. Signals chunky, playful and friendly-technical. Distinctive; good for brand moments and buttons. |

### Techie

**Signals:** engineered, precise, machine-made. Curves are squared off and corners may be cut. The voice of instruments, hardware and science fiction.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Bai Jamjuree | 6 weights, 200–700, italics | Squared rounds on a clean, orderly frame. Signals technical and understated. Mild enough for running text. |
| Chakra Petch | 5 weights, 300–700, italics | Squared forms with tapered, cut corners. Signals gaming, motorsport and heads-up displays. For display and labels; tiring in paragraphs. |
| IBM Plex Sans | Variable 100–700, width 75–100, italics | A grotesque frame with engineered details: squared terminals, right-angled notches, distinctive `a`, `g` and `l`. Signals corporate-technical and rational, with a little character. Strong for data-heavy interfaces and documentation. |
| Rajdhani | 5 weights, 300–700, no italics | Condensed and squared, with open, clipped corners. Signals futuristic and compact. For capital labels, numbers and dashboards. |

## Rounded Sans

**Structure:** stroke ends and corners are rounded off.

**Signals:** soft, safe and playful. Rounding lowers seriousness, so keep it for subjects where gentleness is the point.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Nunito | Variable 200–1000, italics | A balanced sans with fully rounded terminals. Signals gentle and friendly. Suits wellbeing and education; too soft to carry authority. |
| Varela Round | 400 only, no italics | Soft and even in a single weight. Signals simple and kind. With no bold, hierarchy has to come from size alone. |

## Humanist Serifs

**Structure:** modelled on book types of the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, also called old-style. The stress is diagonal, contrast is moderate, serifs are bracketed, and the italics are calligraphic.

**Signals:** literature, scholarship, craft and permanence. Warmth with gravity.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Alegreya | Variable 400–900, italics | Dynamic and calligraphic with a varied rhythm, designed for literature. Signals narrative and history. Suits long reading and cultural projects; it has an assertive personality. |
| Cardo | 400, 700, italic in 400 only | A classical revival built for scholars, with a broad character set. Signals academic and antiquarian. Light on screen at small sizes. |
| Crimson Pro | Variable 200–900, italics | A book face in the old-style tradition redrawn for screens, even in texture, with elegant italics. Signals thoughtful and literary. Suits long reading, and delicate display in its light weights. |
| EB Garamond | Variable 400–800, italics | A revival of sixteenth-century Garamond types. Signals classical and humane. The small lowercase needs larger sizes on screen. |

## Transitional Serifs

**Structure:** the eighteenth-century rationalisation of the old-style model. Stress is close to vertical, contrast is sharper, proportions are more regular and serifs are crisper.

**Signals:** composed authority. Journalism, institutions and law: formal without ornament.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Instrument Serif | 400 only, italic | A narrow, sharp display serif. Signals editorial chic with a retro edge. Display only. It is strongly tied to a current fashion in landing pages, which dates it. |
| Libre Caslon Text | 400, 700, italic in 400 only | A sturdy Caslon tuned for body text on screen. Signals established and dependable, in the manner of traditional print. (Caslon is historically old-style; it sits here for its role as a formal text face.) |
| Newsreader | Variable 200–800, optical size 6–72, italics | Made for reading news on screen. The optical size axis shifts contrast from caption to headline; serifs are crisp and pointed. Signals journalistic and calm. Text and display from one family. |
| Source Serif 4 | Variable 200–900, optical size 8–60, italics | Even, clear and slightly sturdy, in an eighteenth-century French manner. Signals level-headed and professional. Reliable for long text; pairs naturally with Source Sans 3. |

## Contemporary Serifs

**Structure:** recent designs made for screens. The lowercase is large, counters are open, serifs are sturdy, and details are often simplified or faceted.

**Signals:** serious content in a present-day voice. Credible but approachable.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Faustina | Variable 300–800, italics | Compact and slightly angular with crisp details, designed for editorial work. Signals articulate and modern-literary. Text that also makes interesting headlines. |
| Merriweather | Variable 300–900, optical size 18–144, width 87–112, italics | Very large lowercase, sturdy serifs and a robust texture, made for screen reading. Signals solid, earnest and comfortable. Good for dense text; heavy in colour. |
| Noticia Text | 400, 700, italics | Leans towards a slab, with a large lowercase and firm, squared details, made for news on screen. Signals matter-of-fact and practical. For small text; two weights only. |
| PT Serif | 400, 700, italics | Transitional structure with humanist terminals and wide language coverage, from a public type project. Signals official and sober. For body text. |
| Piazzolla | Variable 100–900, optical size 8–30, italics | Compact, expressive forms whose curves are built from facets, made for dense layouts. Signals energetic and sharp. For text and punchy headlines. |

## Modern Serifs

**Structure:** also called Didone. Stress is vertical, the contrast between thick and thin is extreme, and serifs are unbracketed hairlines.

**Signals:** fashion, luxury, ceremony and drama. The hairlines disappear at small sizes, so this is display type.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Bodoni Moda | Variable 400–900, optical size 6–96, italics | A Didone with optical sizes; hairlines sharpen as the optical size rises. Signals couture and ceremony. Use large and set the optical size to match; not for body text. |
| Abril Fatface | 400 only, no italics | A heavy "fat face" in the style of nineteenth-century advertising, with extreme contrast and ball terminals. Signals theatrical and flamboyant. For one or two words at large size. |

## Humanist Slab

**Structure:** block serifs on a humanist frame, keeping some variation in stroke thickness.

**Signals:** sturdy, honest and workmanlike, with warmth. Authority that is not stiff.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Bitter | Variable 100–900, italics | Low contrast, large lowercase and sturdy slabs, designed for comfortable screen reading. Signals grounded and practical. For text and headings. |

## Grotesque Slab

**Structure:** heavy block serifs on a grotesque frame, in the manner of nineteenth-century poster and wood type.

**Signals:** loud, vintage and showy: playbills, signage, Americana. Statement type for short lines.

No fonts are listed in this classification yet.

## Geometric Slab

**Structure:** strokes of one thickness with slab serifs of the same thickness, on geometric construction.

**Signals:** constructed, mechanical and blunt, with a retro-modern flavour. For headings and accents, not paragraphs.

No fonts are listed in this classification yet.

## Monospace

**Structure:** every character occupies the same width.

**Signals:** code, terminals, data and typewriters. Raw, unprocessed, technically honest. Useful for aligning figures and for tags; tiring for paragraphs. In a non-technical design, a monospace adds a technical voice, so ask whether the main family's tabular numerals would do instead.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Fira Code | Variable 300–700, no italics | A monospace with programming ligatures that merge sequences such as `->` and `!=`. Signals developer tooling. For code; the ligatures can alter ordinary labels, so check them. |
| Inconsolata | Variable 200–900, width 50–200, no italics | A humanist monospace with soft curves and a width axis. Signals crafted and quiet, a classic terminal. |
| Roboto Mono | Variable 100–700, italics | Neutral and wide, with clearly distinguished `0`/`O` and `1`/`l`. Signals plain and utilitarian. |
| JetBrains Mono | Variable 100–800, italics | Tall lowercase and narrower letters, built for long sessions in a code editor. Signals contemporary and precise. Fits tight columns. |

## Blackletter

**Structure:** the broken, angular pen script of medieval Europe.

**Signals:** medieval, gothic and ecclesiastical, and by later association newspaper mastheads, heavy music and brewing. A very strong flavour; for short display lines only.

| Font | Available | Character and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Grenze | Variable 100–900, italics | A hybrid between roman and blackletter, kept readable for present-day readers. Signals gothic flavour with legibility. For headlines and short passages. |
| Texturina | Variable 100–900, optical size 12–72, italics | Based on textura blackletter but opened up, with many contextual alternates. Signals dense, ornate and dramatic. Display only. |
